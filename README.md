# Savora Customer Journey Analytics

## Customer Journey Analytics & Conversion Strategy

### Business Context

Savora is a savings and investment platform where customers progress through a journey from sign up and onboarding to funding their accounts and ultimately making an investment.

The business challenge was to understand why many customers who had successfully funded their accounts were not progressing to investment. The analysis investigated the customer journey to identify where conversion was breaking down, when customers were most likely to invest, and where Savora could intervene.

### Business Question

How can Savora convert more funded customers into investors?

### Analytical Approach

The analysis examined:

1. Customer funnel progression from sign up to investment
2. Time to first investment
3. Investment conversion across customer segments
4. Financial behaviour of investors and funded non investors
5. Investment product interaction
6. Investment abandonment
7. Hypothesis testing

### Key Findings

The largest conversion gap occurs between funding and investment.

Out of 12,000 sign ups, 10,841 completed KYC, 9,729 linked their bank, 7,706 successfully funded their accounts, and 998 completed an investment.

This represents:

1. 64.2% sign up to funding conversion
2. 13.0% funding to investment conversion
3. 8.3% sign up to investment conversion

This left 6,708 funded non investors.

The analysis found evidence that some funded non investors showed investment interest. 2,046 customers, or 30.5%, interacted with investment products, while 569 customers, or 8.5%, started an investment but did not complete it.

Among the 569 customers who started but did not complete an investment, risk or product uncertainty accounted for 193 cases, flow interruption for 187, and customer abandonment for 189. Risk or product uncertainty and flow interruption together represented 66.8% of recorded abandonment reasons.

The analysis also identified a potential first 30 day conversion window. 966 of 998 investors, or 96.8%, made their first investment within 30 days of funding, with the largest individual period being days 22 to 30 at 28.1%.

Investment conversion also varied across customer groups. Referral customers recorded 11.7% conversion, customers aged 50+ recorded 13.8%, and customers earning ₦1m+ recorded 15.9% within their respective groups.

### Hypotheses

**H1: Funded non investors primarily use Savora for saving.**

**Result: Not supported.**

Repeat deposit behaviour was almost identical between funded non investors at 54.8% and investors at 54.1%. The data therefore does not provide sufficient evidence that different deposit behaviour explains the investment gap.

**H2: Some funded non investors have investment intent but fail to complete.**

**Result: Supported.**

Evidence included investment product interaction among 30.5% of funded non investors, investment starts among 8.5%, and the recorded abandonment patterns.

### Business Insight

The funding to investment gap is not solely a lack of awareness problem. Some funded customers engage with investment products and progress into the investment process before failing to complete it.

The evidence therefore points to an opportunity to improve what happens after funding rather than relying exclusively on acquiring more customers.

### Recommendations

The analysis recommends:

1. Improving the investment completion journey by investigating flow interruptions, product information, risk clarity, guidance, and unnecessary complexity
2. Testing targeted educational engagement with funded customers within the first 30 days
3. Supporting funded customers who show investment interest through clearer product and risk information and a smoother investment journey
4. Using controlled testing before treating the recommended interventions as proven drivers of conversion

### Measurement

Success should be measured through:

1. Funding to investment conversion
2. Investment completion rate
3. 30 day investment conversion
4. Investment abandonment rate
5. Performance between control and test groups

### Important Limitation

The analysis identifies areas for intervention and testing, but does not establish causation where the data only shows association.

Segment differences do not prove that age, income, or acquisition channel causes higher conversion. Recorded abandonment reasons do not prove that a particular factor caused abandonment. The first 30 days represent a potential intervention window that should be validated through testing.

The current dataset also does not provide enough evidence to quantify financial return, so no specific revenue or investment value uplift is claimed.
